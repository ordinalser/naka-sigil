2　How the guide's claims were checked

ClaimEvidence, and how far it reaches
All inscribed NAKA pieces sit at one NUMS addressThe NUMS point was recomputed following BIP341. In all 26 of NAKA's child inscription transactions, the outputs to this address equal the inscriptions in the transaction, 3,327 in total. The parent isn't included; this counts outputs and is not a full Ordinals replay
The 21M SHNAKAS premine sits at the same addressThe premine's Runestone: Pointer=1, Premine=2100000000000000, Divisibility=8. Output 1 is this NUMS address and was unspent when read. Field meanings checked against the Runes specification
A leaked unlock signature rebuilds the vault, vault BTC includedThe official client holds the fixed message and the derivation: seed = SHA-256("shord/seed/v1" ‖ sig), then shord/spend, shord/view, shord/return, shord/payout, shord/btc, shord/listing and shord/bonus. The vault's withdrawal signs with keys from shord/btc and shord/payout, so a leaked signature exposes the vault's own BTC too. The wallet's own keys are not among them
A confirmed BTC payment is not a completed saleBitcoin script and SHORD validation are separate layers. The protocol document says a BUY that pays wrongly is rejected, and the spent lock closes the listing
The platform can close a listing but can't take the note or the moneyNAKA is a single-key collection: the listing lock's internal key, lockKey, is the platform's key (scripts.mjs; PROTOCOL-v1.md, Listing lock: spending the lock is how the platform closes a listing). Taking the note back needs a CLAIM signed with the seller's return key, and the price goes to the seller's payout script. The 1,008-block relative lock counts from the block that confirms the lock
Nobody can spend from the art's addressThe address's key is the BIP341 NUMS point with no script tree (recomputed, matching the script on chain). "Nobody knows the private key" rests on the usual cryptographic assumptions
Sending to yourself also breaks the current weekThe client's start-week and end-week logic for each note (see card 07). A note made by a reward claim starts in the week it was made, so a normal claim doesn't break the week
A base claim keeps the number private; a bonus claim shows itThe client's code for building base and bonus claims; the bonus carries the number
Private pieces are not private BTCVault receipts and withdrawals use ordinary Bitcoin outputs. One-time addresses are not a mixing pool
The 20,000-sat fee examplecollection has saleFeeBps=200 and saleFeeMin=1000. It is arithmetic, not a market price
NAKA still uses old-style listingscollection shows listings3.active=false for NAKA, and every NAKA listing lock in state is 330 sats. Trollbox and COMMEMORATIVE COINS have LIST3 on, with a 1% fee and a 1% royalty
How weights worknaka-sigil's rules audit: a 100% base; trait floors at 1.25, 1.5, 2 and 3 times the collection floor add 25%, 50%, 75% and 100%; a number bonus; a 400% cap; rescored every block, matched piece by piece against the official weights endpoint. A private claim pays the 100% base; showing the number pays the full weight

The forgotten-PIN text says the 24 words, or importing again from UniSat or Xverse, bring the vault back. The restore option reads "The 24 words of a Shielded Vault", and the security note "On a phone, use your browser's Add to Home Screen to keep this wallet as an app": the Shielded Vault opens on a phone or in a browser, and the 24 words give the same vault in either

What the digest coversstateDigest in indexer.mjs (digest v1): tree root, leaf count, the nullifier set, minted numbers, and each listing's number, state, credits and price
The notes need no backup; the keys bring them backThe envelope format in envelope.mjs: mints, transfers, purchases and claims carry an ephemeral public key and an 88-byte ciphertext whose plaintext is the piece, the credits and the two random values (core.mjs). When the official client scans for notes, it opens each ciphertext with the view key; batch reward notes, which have none, take their random values from the previous note, and the commitment is checked. The client is a computation library with no code that uploads a vault; whether the site's pages store anything themselves wasn't checked
A wrong ciphertext leaves the receiver unable to find the notePROTOCOL-v1.md, "Still open": the circuit doesn't check a transfer's ciphertext; in every paid flow the receiver makes the new note
The public code can't recompute the full live ledgerThe public code's message types stop at 6 (MINTN) and anything else fails to parse; the live client also has rewards 7–10, LIST3 11 and SPLIT 12. Who computes the commitmentThe Notes section of PROTOCOL-v1.md: notes from a mint, purchase or claim are built by the receiver, who publishes only inner and a ciphertext, and "the indexer computes cm from the public assetId and ticket count". In a private transfer the sender's vault computes cmOut and puts it in the envelope, and the circuit proves it matches
What an indexer stores, and what it can't tell after a private sendThe engine fields in indexer.mjs: the record tree, the nullifier set, every block's root and leaf count, the note list (position, commitment, epk, ciphertext), mints, listings and lock positions. checkpoint.mjs states that a loaded engine equals, field for field, one replayed from the start block. A private transfer (mode 0) has assetIdPub = 0 among its public inputs, so the indexer gets only cmOut
While listed the piece is tied to the lock, which can be spent two waysThe Listing lock section of PROTOCOL-v1.md: the lock is P2TR(internal = lockKey, leaf = "<1008> CSV DROP <returnKey> CHECKSIG"); the platform co-signs only a transaction that pays correctly; a cancel carries the seller's CLAIM in the same transaction; a lock spent without a valid BUY closes the listing; after 1,008 blocks the seller can spend the lock alone. Once closed, the seller can send a CLAIM on its own at any time, with no wait: the document says "A lock spent with neither leaves it CLOSED until the seller publishes CLAIM", and indexer.mjs accepts a CLAIM for a CLOSED listing, or an ACTIVE one whose lock this transaction spends. A LIST must make the lock in the same transaction (otherwise "listing lock output missing"); the indexer doesn't check how many sats it holds. One transaction can carry several envelopes: the document's Relay batches section has the relay put a window's envelopes into one transaction (so the lead of chapter 05's second page no longer says one transaction, one envelope per step)
Sale proceeds land at fresh addresses the vault can find againThe official client: the seller's payout script is derived from shord/payout and the listing nullifier, and the vault matches the payout scripts of sold listings to find its own sales

3　Public code and the live version

The public protocol code is shielded-ordinals; this guide was checked against commit 3cdbdfdf of 12 October 2026. The live interfaces and client already have rewards, splits and more; the guide follows the live material and doesn't use the older version to argue against newer features.

The team says newer code will be published. That is their plan; this guide hasn't verified it and can't promise a date. A full independent replay also needs the matching rules, verification parameters and activation settings.

The live reward service was not reproduced independently. The voting service's code never reaches the browser, so only its public records can be checked: the tally and de-duplication match, eligibility can only be sampled (the voting row in section 2). The limits found in the old public holder circuit offline can't be presented as a flaw in live voting.

The guide also used naka-sigil's public code and documents (rules, snapshots, reward checks), reading only code and documents, never anyone's holdings or wallet.

6　What this guide is and isn't

It is community learning material that explains how SHORD works and what to watch for when using it. It is not a security audit of the protocol, the wallets, the parameters or the live services.

7　Details left out of the guide

These were checked but add little on a first encounter with SHORD:

  • Week 1 breakdown: by the saved week 1 data, the claimable ceiling was about 220,370.72, at least 46,269.28 below the nominal pool. Private transfers can't all be matched to numbers, so this isn't an exact network-wide figure.
  • Pool versus ceiling: the 78 weekly pools plus three fixed pouches of 80 at the start total 20,798,160, which is 201,840 below the 21 million etched. The rules assign the difference nowhere and the engine only requires the total to stay within 21 million (“pool exceeds the premine”), so it will never be issued; the runes at NUMS stay put, and this is no burn.
  • Proving speed: PROTOCOL-v1.md gives about 0.8 to 1.0 seconds for the Spend(20) circuit, on a particular version and machine, not necessarily yours.
  • Repricing and offers: the official site shows such interactions; how the server matches them wasn't fully reproduced.
  • Name registration: first valid inscription wins, and the to field maps to one address. The target address doesn't confirm with a signature, moving a name inscription doesn't change the address, and it doesn't prove identity.
  • shield/unshield, auctions, burning for allocation spots: each needs complete rules for entry, exit, settlement and deduplication. Ordinary asset entry and exit isn't complete in the current public version, and the spot rules lack material that can be reproduced.
